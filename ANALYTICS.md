# Analytics and ad-grant measurement plan

**Status:** proposed, not implemented. Written 2026-09-04, revised 2026-09-04
after review.

**Scope:** all three Speak Nigeria web properties, not just this repo. It lives
here because this is the largest of the three codebases and the one whose build
step has to carry the tag. The other two repos are `~/Dev/website`
(speaknigeria.org) and `~/Dev/website-games` (games.speaknigeria.org).

## Why this exists

Google Ad Grants has been approved for the nonprofit, covering yorubadict.com,
speaknigeria.org and games.speaknigeria.org. We intend to advertise
**yorubadict.com** and **games.speaknigeria.org**.

**What we are buying traffic for is use of the free resources.** People playing
the games, and people finding words in the dictionary. That is the mission, and
it is the thing the campaigns exist to produce. Course enrollment is revenue
that sustains the work, but it is not what the grant is for, and it should not
shape the measurement plan.

Ad Grants requires working conversion tracking as a condition of keeping the
account, and independently of that requirement we currently cannot answer basic
questions about how any of the three sites are used. Both problems need solving
before any ad spend, and the second one is the more valuable of the two.

Verified by fetching the live sites and grepping the repos, not from memory:

| Property | Analytics in repo | Analytics live |
|---|---|---|
| yorubadict.com | none | **Cloudflare Web Analytics** (`cloudflareinsights.com/beacon.min.js`) |
| games.speaknigeria.org | none | **nothing** |
| speaknigeria.org | none | **nothing** |

The Cloudflare beacon appears in no repo. The only scripts in our own source are
`english-relevance.js` and `app.js`. Cloudflare is injecting the beacon at the
edge, which means Web Analytics was switched on for that Pages project in the
dashboard by hand.

That is worth recording as a lesson: we spent a long time unable to say what we
were running, precisely because the answer was in a dashboard and not in a file.
**Everything proposed below goes in the repos**, not in the Cloudflare UI.

(The `email-decode.min.js` script on the other two sites is also
Cloudflare-injected, but it is email obfuscation, not analytics.)

So: two of three properties are dark, including one of the two we are about to
buy traffic for.

## 2. The three properties, technically

### 2.1 yorubadict.com — Sọ̀rọ̀ Sókè

A Yorùbá ↔ English dictionary built from Wiktionary data via Kaikki. Cloudflare
Pages, `public/` is the deploy root, no frontend build step.

**The page counts, exactly.** An earlier draft of this document used 8,686,
which is not any of the real numbers. Counted from the working tree:

| Thing | Count |
|---|---|
| Entries in `data/entries.json` | 6,273 |
| Sense pages, `/yo/<form>/<sense-slug>` | 6,273 (one per entry) |
| Spelling pages, `/yo/<spelling>` | 4,343 |
| — of those, ambiguous (two or more words share the spelling) | 829 |
| — of those, single-word | 3,514 |
| Total `.html` under `public/yo/` | 10,616 |
| URLs in `sitemap.xml` | 7,108 (6,273 senses + 829 spellings + 6 written pages) |

The gap between 10,616 files and 7,108 sitemap URLs is the 3,514 single-word
spelling pages, which are real pages but carry a canonical pointing at their one
sense page, so they are deliberately kept out of the sitemap. The 829 ambiguous
spelling pages are self-canonical. **That distinction turns out to decide where
word-level ads should land — see §6.1.**

**Shape that matters for tracking:**

- **Prerendered pages** are generated by `build/lib/prerender.mjs`, which
  substitutes the entry body into the `<!--PRERENDER-BODY-->` marker in
  `public/index.html`. `_redirects` is generated by the same script from
  `RETIRED_PAGES` in `public/page-render.js` and the `retired` list in
  `data/url-slugs.json`. There is deliberately no catch-all rewrite — every real
  address is a real file.
- **It is a client-side SPA despite that.** `public/app.js` installs
  `interceptLinks` on `document` and `handleRoute` on `popstate`. A prerendered
  page is served on first hit and then hydrates; every internal navigation after
  that is `pushState`, with no document load. **This breaks the default
  behaviour of every analytics tool** — one pageview per visit, no matter how
  many entries someone reads. Addressed in §6.1.
- **All search is client-side.** `public/data/entries.json` (~1.4 MB) is fetched
  deliberately *after* first paint (see the LCP note in `app.js`), then
  `search()` runs locally. Three modes cycle through `SEARCH_MODES`:
  `both`, `yoruba`, `english`. No search request ever leaves the browser.
- **A service worker** (`public/sw.js`) caches the shell and the data, keyed on
  the build stamp in `data/version.json`. The dictionary works fully offline.
  **Navigations are network-first** (`sw.js` fetch handler): the cache is the
  `catch` branch, and `version.json` is never cached. Only subresources are
  cache-first. An online returning visitor therefore still generates edge
  requests for pages. This matters in §4, where an earlier draft got it wrong.
- `public/_headers` puts everything on `max-age=0, must-revalidate` except
  `/fonts/*`, because `app.js`, `style.css` and `entries.json` are not
  fingerprinted. Adding a tag does not change this, but note that a tag change
  means regenerating every prerendered page.
- **Written pages:** `/about`, `/speak-nigeria`, `/language-of-connections`,
  `/building-blocks`, `/contribute`, plus a data-quality panel.

**Interaction affordances on an entry page** (all rendered by
`public/entry-render.js`, all delegated from one listener on `entryContent`
because the markup is rebuilt via `innerHTML` on every render):

| Affordance | Class / hook | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Relation pills | `.relation-pill` | Etymology parent/derived, dialect forms, related terms |
| Building-block pills | `[data-search-form]` | Component morphemes; clicking runs a search for that spelling |
| Alternatives expander | `.pill-more` | Reveals every candidate when a spelling is ambiguous |
| Hedge explainer | `.info-toggle` | Why a "Possibly used in" list is hedged |
| Dialect panel | `<details class="dialect-panel">` | Dialectal synonyms |
| Sibling rows | `.sibling-row` | Other entries sharing a spelling |
| Source link | `.dialect-source` | Outbound to Wiktionary |

Following a building-block pill is the project's whole thesis — Yorùbá builds
larger words from smaller ones, and tracing that path is the thing the site does
that nothing else does well. It is therefore the most meaningful thing we can
measure here.

**There is no audio in this repo.** No audio files, no TTS, no pronunciation
playback anywhere. Any plan that treats "played the pronunciation" as a
dictionary conversion is wrong.

### 2.2 games.speaknigeria.org

Cloudflare Pages, `public/` only deploys, plain HTML/CSS/vanilla JS, no build
step, cache-busted by hand as `app.js?v=N`. Used during live Speak Nigeria
Yorùbá lessons, so traffic is teacher-driven and bursty.

**Two games ship:**

- **`public/phonics/`** (637 lines) — match syllable buttons in order to build a
  word, with an image and full-word audio.
- **`public/tones/`** (1,020 lines) — hear a word, pick high/mid/low for each
  syllable. Its High/Mid/Low reference buttons are synthesised from `TONE_MODEL`
  rather than recorded.

`public/dev/story/` (tap-the-story) is in trial and does not deploy.

**Shared data:** both read `../phonics/vocab.json`, `syllables.json` and
`sessions.json` — one vendored copy, generated by the separate
`yoruba-student-dict` repo. `sessions.json` holds **30 levels**, each with
`levelId`, `category`, `words` and `validSpeakers`. Word counts run **3 to 12,
median 8** — small enough that finishing a level in one sitting is normal, which
§7 depends on. Categories are `themed`, `syllable_reinforcement`,
`tone_pattern` and `endless_practice`. Both games filter to `ALLOWED_SPEAKERS`,
and tones replaces the `tone_pattern` levels wholesale. Word audio and images
are served from `gamemedia.speaknigeria.org` (R2).

**Runtime state, identical in both:** `activeLevels`, `currentLevelIndex`,
`currentWordIndex`, `currentWord`, `maxSlots`, `isTransitioning`, plus
`queue` (phonics) or `picks` / `isSolved` (tones), and `showingHint`.

**No URL state at all.** Neither game reads or writes the address bar. There is
no way to link to a level or a playlist. This blocks share links (§6.2) and is
the one real feature build in this plan.

**Answer flow — and one asymmetry that matters:**

- **tones** — `handleTonePick()` fills `picks`, and when all are filled calls
  `checkAnswer()` (`tones/app.js:915`), which has explicit correct *and* wrong
  branches. The wrong branch marks each incorrect card, plays `playWrong()`,
  clears, and lets them retry. Both outcomes already exist as distinct code
  paths.
- **phonics** — `checkWinCondition()` (`phonics/app.js:564`) is a bare `if
  (isMatch)` with no `else`. A full but incorrect queue falls through and does
  nothing. **There is no wrong-answer code path to hook.** Instrumenting phonics
  failures requires adding one, which is a real code change, not just a
  `track()` call.

**Deliberately no score, no lives, no penalty** in either game (see the comment
above `playWrong` in `tones/app.js`). Tracking must not smuggle a scoring model
back in; these are measurements for us, not feedback for the learner.

**Other instrumentable actions:** playlist selection (`selectPlaylist`), hint
toggle (tone dots in phonics, tone melody in tones), skip word ("Skipping
word…"), previous/next word, full-word audio replay, per-syllable audio replay,
fullscreen toggle.

**No telemetry and no `localStorage` today.** There is no returning-learner
identity of any kind.

### 2.3 speaknigeria.org

Cloudflare Pages, **repo root is the deploy root** (no `public/`), six static
pages, no build step, DNS at Squarespace: `index`, `about`, `languages`,
`courses`, `resources`, `get-involved`.

**Every form is an external Google Form** — `forms.gle/WmNKTMYTat3cAFUo8` plus
two `docs.google.com/forms/...` links, all three in `courses.html`. Course
enrollment, course information requests and volunteer sign-ups all leave our
domain, and we can only see the click that leaves. Google Forms cannot redirect
to a custom confirmation URL, so no configuration fixes this.

This is a real gap, but it is **not on the critical path**, because we are not
advertising this property and enrollment is not what the grant is buying. It is
worth fixing on its own merits, at its own pace. See §9.3.

### 2.4 Cross-property traffic is real

- **dictionary → nonprofit:** 17 links to `speaknigeria.org`, 13 to
  `/courses.html`, 9 to `games.speaknigeria.org/`, 2 to `/about`
- **games → nonprofit and dictionary:** links to every page on speaknigeria.org
  plus `yorubadict.com`
- **nonprofit → both:** `yorubadict.com`, `games.speaknigeria.org`,
  `games.speaknigeria.org/phonics/`

Dictionary → games is the journey we most want to see, since both are ad

## 3. Requirements this has to satisfy

1. **Ad Grants conversion tracking** — at least one meaningful conversion,
   recording at least 1 conversion per month, or the account is deactivated.
2. **Understand the games** — nothing is currently known about how they are
   played, down to how individual words are answered. This has curriculum value
   to Speak Nigeria independent of ads.
3. **Understand the dictionary** — which entries, which searches find nothing,
   whether anyone follows the building-block links.
4. **Open-ended drilling.** We do not know yet what we will want to ask. The
   plan must leave us able to ask new questions of old data, which is a
   constraint on *event properties*, not on event volume. See §5.
5. **See cross-property journeys** — dictionary → games especially.
6. **Free**, and ideally zero-ops.

## 4. Options considered and rejected

Recorded with reasons, including the ones we got wrong along the way.

**GA4 as the default choice** — rejected. Its one genuine advantage here is
automatic cross-domain link decoration, which is a few hours of work to
replicate. Against that: a second tag on every page, a second consent surface,
a second dashboard nobody maintains, and a reporting model built for
e-commerce. Its major advertiser features — remarketing, audience lists — are
unusable to us because Ad Grants serves search text ads only. **GA4 is not
required for Ad Grants**; the Google Ads tag is a separate product and is
sufficient.

**Cookieless aggregate tools — Rybbit, Umami, Plausible** — rejected, and not
because they are bad. They identify nobody across pageviews by design, so
requirements 2 and 4 are impossible with them: no per-word answer detail, no
re-querying old data along a new dimension. No configuration changes that; it is
the thing they are built not to do.

**Self-hosting anything** — rejected on the "free" criterion. Rybbit needs
Docker plus ClickHouse; Umami needs Postgres or MySQL. That is a VPS at
$5–10/month plus backups, upgrades and someone on call. PostHog additionally no
longer supports self-hosting for production.

**Cloudflare zone analytics as a visitor count** — rejected, but for two
reasons rather than the three an earlier draft gave. It counts requests at the
edge with no JavaScript, so every crawler counts (Googlebot, Bingbot, GPTBot,
ClaudeBot, scrapers — for a free dictionary with thousands of indexed pages,
plausibly more traffic than our humans), and "unique visitors" is an IP +
user-agent estimate that reads a whole school behind one NAT as one visitor.
Bot classification is limited on the free plan.

The third reason given — that `sw.js` serves returning visitors entirely from
cache and so generates no edge request — **was wrong**. Navigations are
network-first; the cache is the offline fallback. Only subresources are
cache-first. Returning online visitors do hit the edge. The rejection stands on
the first two reasons; the undercount claim does not.

**Reverse-proxying PostHog ingestion through a Worker on our own domain** —
rejected on ethics. The stated reason for doing it was that `posthog.com` is on
common blocklists and we would otherwise lose data. That is deliberately
defeating a choice the visitor already made. Proxying to reduce third-party data
sharing is defensible; proxying to recover people who opted out is not, and the
second was the actual motive. **We accept the undercount instead** — likely
10–30%, higher among US/UK diaspora, lower on Nigerian mobile. The bias is
roughly stable, so trends and comparisons stay valid even though absolute counts
are low; no method gives a true human count anyway.

**Gating the Google Ads tag on `gclid`** — rejected. It was proposed to limit
tracking to ad visitors, but it breaks delayed conversions: the tag normally
sets a `_gcl_aw` cookie on the landing page that carries attribution for weeks,
so a visitor who clicks an ad, leaves, and returns days later to play a game
still credits the ad. Gated on `gclid`, that second visit loads no tag and the
conversion is invisible. For a language-learning audience, "came back later and
actually used it" is likely our best conversion.

**Offline conversion import via stored `gclid`** — rejected. It works and Google
supports it, but if the motive is recovering people who blocked the tag, it is
the proxy idea in different clothes. The payoff is near zero for us: the policy
needs one conversion a month, and smart bidding at our volume will not notice a
thinner sample.

**PostHog EU region** — rejected in favour of **US**. GDPR attaches to where the
visitor is, not where the data sits, so EU hosting exempts us from nothing and
US hosting creates no exposure by itself — either way we need consent handling
and PostHog's DPA. We are a US nonprofit, and **the region cannot be changed
later** without starting over, so US is the default that matches where the
organisation is. Note the audience itself is unmeasured — see §9.1 — but since
GDPR follows the visitor rather than the data, the region does not turn on it.

**Treating the dictionary's privacy copy as a hard constraint** — set aside as a
deliberate decision. `public/page-render.js:168` currently reads:

> This dictionary is free and non-commercial. It carries no advertising, sells
> nothing, and collects nothing about you — after your first visit the whole
> dictionary is on your device and your searches never leave it.

This was aspirational copy rather than a considered commitment, and is not a
constraint we are choosing to keep. But it is *live copy that will become
false*, so shipping any tag here requires revising it in the same change. See
§9.3.

An earlier draft defended the second half of that sentence on the grounds that
search is genuinely client-side, so queries really do not leave the device. That
defence is withdrawn. It is boasting about an implementation detail nobody is
asking about, and keeping it would rule out capturing the one search signal
worth having (§9.3). Replace the whole passage with something plain and true.

## 5. Proposed stack

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| Product + web analytics | **PostHog Cloud, free tier, US region, one project, all three domains** |
| Ad grant conversions | **Google Ads tag, site-wide, under consent mode.** No GA4. |
| Organic search queries | **Google Search Console** on all three domains |
| Baseline | Existing Cloudflare Web Analytics beacon on the dictionary — leave it alone |

**Why PostHog:** it is the only option that is genuinely free with no server to
run, deep enough for the game questions (funnels, retention, session replay, SQL
over event properties), and capable of visitor-level cross-domain journeys.

**Configuration:**

- **One project, not three.** Cross-site journeys only exist if events land in
  the same place. Distinguish with a `site` property on every event.
- **Autocapture OFF from the start.** An earlier draft turned it on because "we
  do not yet know what to instrument," which §6 contradicts — §6 names the
  events, the functions and the line numbers. More importantly, autocapture
  cannot serve requirement 4 *on this codebase*: it identifies elements by CSS
  selector and DOM position, and both `entry-render.js` and the games rebuild
  their markup with `innerHTML` on every render. The selectors are not stable
  across renders, let alone across a redesign. Autocapture here buys noise that
  cannot be drilled into, at the price of most of the event budget.
- **Open-ended drilling comes from properties, not autocapture.** Put every
  dimension we might later want onto deliberate events, and query with SQL. This
  is the actual answer to requirement 4, and it is why §6 events carry as many
  properties as they do. Properties are effectively free; events are what we are
  metered on.
- **Session replay on, sampled, games only.** For "how do people actually play,"
  ten replays of a real classroom session teach more than a month of counts.
- **`cross_subdomain_cookie`** covers speaknigeria.org ↔ games.speaknigeria.org
  ↔ gamemedia.speaknigeria.org for free — same registrable domain.
  **yorubadict.com is separate.** Decorate the highest-intent cross-domain links
  only — dictionary → games, and games → dictionary — rather than all ~30
  inventoried in §2.4. PostHog's referrer reporting already tells us *that* the
  other journeys happen; decoration only adds stitching them to one visitor,
  which is worth paying for on the two links we actually care about.
- **No reverse proxy.** See §4.
- **Consent handling, geo-gated to the EEA and UK.** Google consent mode v2 in
  `denied` state by default, upgraded to `granted` for visitors outside the
  EEA/UK, with a banner shown only to those inside it. This is the standard
  arrangement and it is settled here rather than left open — see §9.1 for the
  reasoning and the mechanism. One shared file, copied by a script, not three
  hand-maintained copies.

**Volume:** the free tier is 1M events/month plus ~5k session replays. An
earlier draft put autocapture at "roughly 20–50 events per session → ~800k,
inside the cap but not comfortably." That arithmetic is wrong at the top of its
own range: 20k sessions × 50 events is exactly 1M. With explicit events only,
the same 20k sessions run about 200k/month, which leaves real headroom and room
for replay.

**Where the snippet goes:** the dictionary's prerender template in
`build/lib/prerender.mjs` (then a normal build run regenerates the pages); three
files by hand in the games repo; six by hand in the website repo. Not the
Cloudflare dashboard.

**How it loads is a constraint, not a detail.** `test/paint-budget.test.mjs`
drives a real browser and fails the build if anything heavy is requested before
the largest paint. Its own header records a case where the same code scored 65
instead of 99 because of load ordering, and `app.js` already holds a deliberate
gate that keeps `entries.json` and `search-index.json` behind the paint. Two
third-party tags dropped into the template head are precisely what that test
exists to catch.

So the tags load **after the paint, through the same gate**, and
`npm run test:paint` is part of shipping this — not a check we run afterwards
and hope. Consequences to design around:

- Events fired before the tag is ready must queue and flush, or early
  interactions are lost. PostHog's snippet stubs its own queue; the Ads tag
  needs `gtag` stubbed the same way.
- Consent resolution (§9.1) is a `fetch` and lands on its own schedule.
  Default `denied`, queue, then flush on resolution.
- The dictionary is the only property with a paint budget under test. The games
  and the website can load normally, but the games are used on classroom
  connections, so the same discipline is worth keeping.

## 6. Event schema

One `track()` wrapper per property, so call sites know nothing about either
service and swapping tools later touches one function:

```js
function track(name, props) {
  posthog.capture(name, { site: 'yorubadict', ...props });
}
```

### 6.1 yorubadict.com

**Pageviews need manual handling.** Because of the SPA router in §2.1, the
default one-pageview-per-load will report a single view for a visit that read
twenty entries. Either set `capture_pageview: 'history_change'` or capture
explicitly inside `handleRoute`. This is the single most important
project-specific detail in this document.

**Where a word ad should land.** The question was whether to advertise
individual words, and if so whether an ad should land on a word's page — given
that a spelling without tone marks is often not one word. It should not land on
a sense page, but it does not need a prefilled search either, because the
landing page already exists and is already built:

- `/yo/<spelling>` for an **ambiguous** spelling is a prerendered
  disambiguation page. `public/yo/fa.html` is titled *"fa — 2 Yorùbá words"*
  and its description reads: *"2 different Yorùbá words are written fa once tone
  marks are removed: fà, fá. Tone changes what a word means."* There are 829 of
  these, all self-canonical and all in the sitemap.
- This beats a `?q=` search route on every axis. It is real HTML with its
  content in the markup, so it does not wait on the 1.4 MB `entries.json` fetch
  the way a prefilled search would. Its content *is* the disambiguation. And it
  puts the tone argument — our actual differentiator — on the landing page
  instead of one click in.
- For an **unambiguous** spelling there is nothing to disambiguate, and its
  spelling page canonicals to the one sense anyway, so those ads land on the
  sense page directly.

So: **do not build a search-query route for ads.** Two ad shapes, two landing
patterns, both already deployed.

**Which words, if any.** Not all 829. Start with the 25 entries in
`data/building-blocks.json` — the words the dictionary already identifies as
roots, each with a `buildsCount` between 22 and 58:

| Form | Part of speech | Builds | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ṣe | verb | 58 | to do |
| ilé | noun | 56 | home, house |
| ilẹ̀ | noun | 50 | land, earth, soil |
| ojú | noun | 47 | eye |
| ní | verb | 46 | to have |
| ọmọ | noun | 45 | child |

That set is imperfect: `ní` appears twice with different parts of speech because
the etymology ids are ambiguous, and `ilé` and `ilẹ̀` differ only by a subdot
and a tone mark. Neither problem stops it being the right test set, and the
second is arguably the pitch.

The reason to test these rather than words in general is in §9.2: a single
word's meaning is exactly what an AI answer now supplies at a glance, but a
building block's value is the family it generates, which a glance answer does
not show. `ṣe` is not interesting as "to do." It is interesting as the root of
58 words, and that is a page, not a sentence.

One defect to fix regardless: `handleRoute` has no renderer for `/yo/<spelling>`
(see its own comment), so a direct visit gets the real file but an in-app click
falls through to the welcome page. Ad clicks are direct visits and work today.
In-app links to spelling pages do not.

| Event | Hook | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| `$pageview` | `handleRoute` | path, referrer |
| `entry_view` | `renderEntry` (`app.js:114`) | `entryId`, `pos`, `hasEtymology`, `morphemeCount`, `source`: `search` / `spelling-page` / `relation` / `direct` |
| `spelling_page_view` | `/yo/<spelling>` landing | `spelling`, `candidateCount` |
| `sense_chosen` | `entry_view` whose `source` is `spelling-page` | `spelling`, `entryId`, `candidateCount` |
| `search_settled` | end of a search episode — see below | `query`, `mode`, `resultCount`, `clicked`, `dwellMs`, `topResultIds` (first 3), `refinedFromPrevious` |
| `search_none_matched` | the "none of these" control | `query`, `mode`, `resultCount`, `topResultIds` |
| `building_block_followed` | `[data-search-form]` branch of the `entryContent` handler | `relationType` |
| `relation_followed` | `.relation-pill` via `interceptLinks` | `relationType` (etymology / dialect / related) |
| `alternatives_opened` | `.pill-more` branch | — |
| `dialect_panel_opened` | `<details class="dialect-panel">` toggle | — |
| `hedge_explainer_opened` | `.info-toggle` branch | — |
| `data_quality_opened` | `qualityToggle` listener | — |
| `outbound_click` | `interceptLinks` | `target`: `games` / `speaknigeria` / `courses` / `wiktionary` |
| `pwa_installed` | `appinstalled` | — |

`sense_chosen` is the word-ad conversion: someone landed on a toneless spelling,
saw that it was two or three different words, and picked one. That is the
product working.

**Why `search_settled` rather than one event per search.** `onSearchInput`
debounces at **60ms** (`app.js:766`), which is effectively per keystroke. Typing
`ilé` runs three searches, two of them abandoned by construction. Logging each
one would produce a stream dominated by people typing, at a volume out of
proportion to its value.

So fire once per search *intent*: when the query stops changing for about 1.5
seconds, or on blur, clear, or navigation away. `refinedFromPrevious` marks the
case where the next episode's query is a prefix extension or a diacritic variant
of this one, which is how a genuine end-of-search is told apart from someone
still typing.

**What "results shown, nothing clicked" does and does not tell us.** It is
tempting to read a no-click search as a failed one. It is not, because
[renderResults](public/app.js#L330) already puts the meaning in the row along
with the headword, part of speech, etymology number and any dialect or relation
note. A reader who searches `ilé`, reads "home, house, abode" in the result row
and stops has been answered. `clicked: false` therefore mixes people we
satisfied at a glance with people we failed, and dwell time does not reliably
separate them — both leave quickly.

That ambiguity is irreducible from behaviour alone, which is why
`search_none_matched` exists. Where inference is weak, ask. See §6.4.

### 6.2 games.speaknigeria.org

**Share links need URL routing built first.** Neither game has any URL state
(§2.2), so there is nothing to share. The version worth building is not a
generic "share this site" button but a deep link carrying `levelId` and
category, because traffic is teacher-driven: the thing a teacher wants is to
send a class one specific level. Give the shared URL its own campaign parameter
and the referral loop measures itself, with no cross-domain work.

Be honest about the limit: a share *click* is observable, and whether anyone
received or opened the link is not. The Web Share API gives no success callback
on mobile.

those questions yet, and the properties are what make them askable later.

in the `yoruba-student-dict` pipeline.

and **`level_abandoned` fires on `pagehide` only, not `visibilitychange`** —
these games are played in a classroom, where a teacher switching tabs is normal
and is not abandonment.

### 6.4 Asking the reader: where it earns its place, and where it backfires

Adding a "none of these matched" control raises the obvious next question —
should entries carry a "report an error" control too, for a dictionary whose
whole premise is that anyone can improve it? **No.** The two look like the same
feature and are not, and the difference is worth stating because it decides
several later questions too.

**The test: does the reader know something we cannot find out another way?**

For search, yes, and uniquely. The gap between what someone wanted and what we
hold exists only in their head. No amount of data mining surfaces it, and §6.1
shows that behaviour cannot even reliably tell a satisfied reader from a
disappointed one. Asking is the only instrument that works.

For entry errors, no — and we can show it. The build already produces, every
run, from `data/validation-report.json` and `data/wiktionary-tasks.json`:

| Known defect | Count |
|---|---|
| Unknown referenced words | 6,332 |
| Duplicate normalized spellings | 1,579 |
| Inferred canonical forms | 777 |
| Missing IPA | 374 |
| References needing anchors | 2,865 |
| Curated contribution tasks, with the wikitext to paste | 120 |

That is a machine-generated queue, specific down to the edit each item needs,
already larger than we can clear. Unverified human reports would not add
information to it. They would add triage, competing for the same scarce
attention, and most would duplicate something already on the list.

**Three further reasons it backfires here specifically:**

1. **We cannot fix them.** The words come from Wiktionary. A "report an error"
   control implies we will act, and the honest answer is "edit Wiktionary,"
   which the site already says well: *"A correction made there reaches this
   dictionary at the next refresh, and everything else in the world built on
   Yorùbá Wiktionary at the same time."* A report box quietly contradicts that.
2. **The fix path already exists on every entry.** `entry-render.js:59` links
   each entry to its Wiktionary page. The work is not building a channel; it is
   at most making that link say what it is for.
3. **A channel nobody reads is worse than none.** It is a promise broken
   quietly, and given the 120-item backlog we should not make it.

**The one place reader feedback beats the validator: tone.** A wrong tone mark
parses cleanly, so validation cannot see it, and only a speaker catches it. Tone
accuracy is also the thing we claim to do better than the alternatives. That is
the single class of error where a report carries information we have no other
way to get.

**So the principle: ask where we already admit doubt, not everywhere.** The site
already hedges — "Possibly used in", the `.info-toggle` explainer, the 777
inferred canonical forms. A reader opening that explainer is already weighing a
claim we flagged. A narrow question at that moment is framed, answerable, and
high-signal, in a way an open comment box on 6,273 entries is not. It also
already has an event: `hedge_explainer_opened`.

**What to build, in order:**

1. **`search_none_matched`** — the "none of these" control. Ours alone, no
   upstream, and the triage is reading a ranked list.
2. **Nothing new on entries.** Reword the existing Wiktionary link so it reads
   as the place to go when something looks wrong. Zero triage, and it turns a
   complaint into an upstream fix that reaches everyone downstream of Wiktionary,
   not just us.
3. **Later, only if there is capacity to read it:** a tone-specific control on
   hedged claims. Not a general report box, and not before someone owns the
   queue.

## 7. Mapping events to Ad Grants conversions

Google needs conversions in **Google Ads**, delivered by the Ads tag. Only a
small subset of the above should be conversions at all.

### 7.1 The rule that decides membership

**A conversion must discriminate between a good click and a bad one.** An event
that fires on nearly every click teaches smart bidding nothing — it is a column
of 1s, arithmetically identical to Maximize clicks — *and* it produces the
inflated conversion rate that draws policy review. One test, both problems.

This is why arriving on the ad's own landing page must never be a conversion,
and it is also why `game_start` is not a primary one. If a games ad lands on a
game, `game_start` fires on essentially every non-bounce click.

### 7.2 What the policy actually permits

Checked against the current Ad Grants documentation rather than from memory. The
excluded set is narrow and specific:

> Conversion types such as time spent on site or homepage visits may be added to
> your account, but must be excluded from "Conversions" and use the category of
> 'Other'.

So the prohibited shapes are **duration** and **arrival** — passive metrics. The
acceptable list is more permissive than assumed, and includes donations,
purchases, ticket sales, sign-ups, form submissions, app downloads, **quizzes
completed**, and "engagement with content on your website."

Two consequences. `level_complete` maps almost exactly onto "quizzes completed,"
which makes it the safest primary we have. And a **count threshold** such as
"attempted three words" is not literally prohibited — it is neither a duration
nor an arrival — but it is the closest thing to an engagement proxy on our list,
and Google's own guidance says that when conversion rates look high, grantees
should confirm the cause is a meaningful conversion rather than inflated
engagement tracking. Since `sessions.json` has a median of 8 words per level and
a minimum of 3, almost anyone who attempts three words is on track to finish a
level anyway. The threshold is therefore both the riskiest item and the most
redundant one. **Dropped** as a conversion; kept as Category Other if we want it
in reporting.

Note that none of this is enforced when the tag fires — the Ads tag is a `gtag`
call and cannot see what triggered it. Compliance is judged at account review,
which is why the conservative choice is cheap and a suspension is not.

### 7.3 The conversion set

| Conversion action | Source event | Primary? | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level completed | `level_complete` | **Primary** | One |
| Shared a level | `share_link_created` | **Primary** | One |
| Chose a sense from a spelling | `sense_chosen` | **Primary** | One |
| Followed a building block | `building_block_followed` | **Primary** | One |
| Found something by searching | `search_settled` with `resultCount > 0` | **Primary** | One |
| Game started | `game_start` | Secondary | One |
| Dictionary installed | `pwa_installed` | Secondary | One |
| Course or volunteer form click | `outbound_form_click` | Secondary | One |
| Words attempted ≥ 3 | threshold | Category **Other**, excluded from Conversions | — |
| Time on site | — | Category **Other**, excluded from Conversions | — |

All five primaries are discrete actions that a bounced click does not produce.

**Count: One**, not Every, on all of them. Someone tracing a word family may
click twelve building-block pills in a visit; that is one success, and telling
the bidding algorithm it was twelve teaches it to chase the longest browse.

A conversion action is a **category**, defined once, not a thing defined per
word or per link. One delegated listener covers every entry page. Eight
definitions total.

**Policy obligations to satisfy at account setup:** specific geo-targeting; at
least 2 unique sitelink assets per campaign; 5% account CTR each month (two
consecutive misses deactivates); at least 1 conversion per month; start on
Maximize clicks if necessary but move to Maximize conversions at ~15–30
conversions/30 days (this also lifts the $2 CPC cap); respond to the periodic
program survey from an address a human reads. Also confirm the keyword rules
separately — no single-word and no overly generic keywords.

### 7.4 One account, and what that costs us

Settled: yorubadict.com was approved as an additional domain on the
speaknigeria.org account. Both properties advertise from **one account**.

The consequence that matters is the **5% CTR floor, which pools**. It is judged
on the account, not per campaign, so a campaign with weak click-through drags on
the standing of every other campaign, and two consecutive monthly misses
deactivates the whole account — the games included.

That makes CTR, not budget, the scarce resource, and it sets the risk order for
launches. Everything starts **targeted to the United States only**, which is
also how the policy's requirement for specific geo-targeting is met — see §9.1
for why the geography and the consent decision are one decision.

1. **Games campaigns and the dictionary-as-a-tool campaign first.** Both aim at
   people looking for a thing we have, so their click-through should be healthy.
2. **Word-level ads second, small, and watched.** They are the experiment most
   likely to draw impressions without clicks (§9.2), which is precisely the
   shape that erodes a pooled CTR floor. Cap them to the 25 building blocks,
   check CTR weekly, and pause them rather than the account.
3. **Other countries only after that**, and read §9.1 before adding the UK or
   Ireland: ad clicks from a banner country under-report conversions, so a
   campaign there reads worse than an identical one here.

Budget is not the constraint anywhere in this plan. Grant money is notional and
we will never approach $10k/month. CTR and account standing are the real
limits.

## 9. Decisions and known gaps

### 9.1 Consent: settled, not open

There is a standard answer, and it is **show a banner in the EEA and UK, and
nowhere else.**

What forces it is not primarily the law but Google. Using Google Ads tags means
agreeing to Google's EU user consent policy, which requires consent from EEA and
UK visitors and, since consent mode v2, requires the signal be passed in a form
Google recognises. That obligation is contractual, applies to us the moment the
Ads tag ships, and is the binding constraint regardless of how the legal
questions come out. GDPR points the same way for those visitors: Article 3(2)
reaches a US organisation that monitors the behaviour of people in the EU, which
analytics on a public site is.

Everywhere else the picture is different enough to justify a different default:

- **United States.** The state privacy laws mostly regulate for-profit
  businesses over size thresholds we do not approach. Colorado's is the notable
  one written to cover nonprofits, and its thresholds are also far above our
  traffic. Worth a lawyer's confirmation before we lean on it, but no
  US-specific banner obligation is likely.
- **Nigeria.** The NDPA reaches processing of Nigerian data subjects and
  requires a lawful basis, of which consent is one but not the only one. It is
  also the worst place to take an undercount hit, since Nigerian mobile is the
  audience we most want to see clearly.

So the arrangement is: consent mode defaults to `denied`; a small script
resolves the visitor's country and either shows the banner or calls `update`
with `granted`. Both PostHog and the Ads tag are gated on the same signal.

The mechanism is already available to us and needs no library. Every
Cloudflare-proxied domain serves `/cdn-cgi/trace`, which returns the visitor's
country as a `loc=` line — confirmed working on yorubadict.com. One `fetch`,
parse one line. Order matters: default to `denied`, resolve, then upgrade. Never
the reverse, or EEA visitors are tracked during the round trip.

**How much this costs us depends on an audience we have not measured**, and an
earlier draft asserted it did not. It claimed the great majority of traffic
would be US diaspora and Nigeria and would never see a banner. That was an
assumption borrowed from the region decision above, restated as a fact.

Yorùbá has more than 40 million speakers, overwhelmingly in Nigeria, with
communities in Benin and Togo. The diaspora is largest in the **United
Kingdom** — which the banner covers — along with the United States, Canada and
Ireland, and there is durable interest in Brazil and Cuba where Yorùbá carries
religious tradition rather than heritage.

**The working assumption, until measured: traffic distributed across Nigeria,
the English-speaking diaspora, and to a lesser extent elsewhere.** Not
US-centric, and not EEA-negligible. Search Console will eventually report this
by country, but it needs volume to do so and the sites are far too young — that
answer is months away, not weeks, and the plan should not wait on it.

The UK and Ireland sit inside the English-speaking diaspora *and* inside the
banner, so **a meaningful minority of visitors will see it.** It has to work
well on a phone rather than merely exist; it is not a rounding error we can
design past.

**Decided: basic consent mode, and initial advertising targeted to the United
States only.**

Basic mode blocks every tag until someone accepts, so a visitor who declines or
ignores the notice produces nothing at all — no cookie, no cookieless ping, no
modelled conversion. It is the stricter of Google's two consent-mode
implementations, and it is a real cost: Google models the conversions it cannot
observe, and basic mode leaves it a general model rather than one built for us.

US-only targeting is what makes that cost affordable, and the ordering matters.
It is not an assumption that Europe will be quiet — it is a decision that ad
traffic will not arrive from the banner countries in the first place. **Ad
clicks will not see a banner**, so conversion tracking on paid traffic is
complete and unmodelled. Organic visitors from the UK and elsewhere still see
the notice, and their measurement is still gated behind it; that is a separate
question from campaign reporting and does not affect it.

Two things to revisit, not now:

- **Expanding targeting to the UK or Ireland changes this.** A UK ad click lands
  on a banner, and someone who declines produces no observed conversion at all,
  so a UK campaign's conversion rate would read worse than a US one performing
  identically. Do not compare them as though they were measured the same way,
  and reconsider consent mode before expanding rather than after.
- **Nigeria is likely our largest audience and is not in the initial
  targeting.** That is a deliberate narrow start, not a judgement about where
  the users are. Nigerian visitors see no banner, so conversion tracking there
  is complete whenever we do target it.

### 9.2 Word-level ads: the real question is AI answers, not budget

An earlier draft asked whether the dictionary is worth advertising at all, on
the grounds that organic search might be worth more than a capped grant. That
framing was wrong. Grant money is notional and we will never spend it; there is
no budget being traded away. Value added is the criterion, and the constraint is
CTR (§7.4), not spend.

**Advertising the dictionary itself is a clear yes.** Someone searching for a
Yorùbá dictionary is looking for a tool, that intent is durable, and we have the
tool. Run it.

**Word-level ads are the genuine open question,** and the reason to doubt them
is AI search. "What does *fa* mean in Yorùbá" is exactly the query an AI
overview or a chatbot now answers inline. The click that used to come to a
dictionary does not arrive, and an ad against that query buys impressions
without clicks — which, on a pooled CTR floor, is actively expensive.

The building-blocks set in §6.1 is the sharpest available test of whether any
word ads survive that, because those are the words where a glance answer is
least sufficient. The value of `ṣe` is not "to do"; it is the 58 words built
from it, and a relational map is not something a sentence-long answer delivers.
If word ads work anywhere, they work there. If the building blocks cannot hold
5% CTR, no word ad will, and we stop.

Run it as 25 ads, watched weekly, pausable without touching the rest of the
account.

### 9.3 Still open

**Search query text: settled.** Capture it on `search_settled`, at every result
count. The privacy argument against it has been withdrawn (§4). Cap the length
as ordinary hygiene rather than as a stance. `refinedFromPrevious` does most of
the work the old caveat warned about — typos and diacritic variants are mostly
mid-typing states, and marking them as such is better than discarding the whole
signal. `search_none_matched` is the clean version of the same question, per
§6.4.

**The privacy copy still needs revising** in the same change that ships a tag,
because it will otherwise be false. Plain and accurate, without the boast.

**Google Forms are a measurement dead end.** Enrollment, information requests
and volunteer sign-ups can only be counted as clicks. The fix is to bring the
forms in-house — a Cloudflare Pages Function with Turnstile posting to a
`/thank-you` page. Worth doing on its own merits, but not on this plan's
critical path, since we are not advertising that property.

**Offline use is invisible.** `sw.js` makes the dictionary usable with no
connection, and those sessions will not report. Note this is narrower than an
earlier draft claimed: navigations are network-first, so it costs us genuinely
offline sessions only, not returning visitors generally. Events could be queued
in IndexedDB and flushed on reconnect. Accepted gap for now.

**We accept a 10–30% undercount**, per §4, now likely at the low end of that
range given §9.1. Do not reconcile PostHog against Cloudflare and expect
agreement; they measure different things badly in different directions. For paid
traffic specifically, comparing PostHog sessions against Google Ads' own
server-side click counts gives an honest rough blocker rate without touching
anyone who opted out.
